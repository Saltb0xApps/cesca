the myth

Ariadne gave Theseus a thread.

He went into the labyrinth and slew the Minotaur — but the labyrinth's real cruelty wasn't the monster. It was the walls. Ten thousand corridors, no map, no way back to daylight.

So Ariadne stayed at the entrance and held one end of a long thread. He tied the other end to himself and walked in. When the thing was done, he followed the thread back out.

That's what we do here. You go into your own labyrinth — every thing you make, every passion you've stacked on top of every other passion. Ariadne stands at the entrance and holds the thread. You don't lose yourself, and you find your way back to a clear, true sentence about who you are.

"And she gave him a clew of thread, that he might find his way back through the windings."

— Plutarch, Theseus 19

how it works

Four turns of the thread.

01

Map the mess

You list everything you do, learn, and want to share. We give you a wide taxonomy with hundreds of options — or write your own. Don't tidy.

02

Find the thread

What ties it all together? If you already feel it, write it. If you don't, we run an ikigai exercise — four prompts whose overlap is, often, the thread.

03

Get suggestions

From your own answers, we propose content pillars, repeating formats, and platforms that fit. You pick what feels right and add what we missed.

04

Stay with it

Every week or two, Ariadne checks in. Five short questions plus a quick log of your platform numbers — so you can see whether the plan is working.
