Investing in 5 minutes

Before you put a dollar anywhere, here's what each option actually is. Tap a card to expand.

🏦 High-Yield Savings (HYSA)

What: A bank account that pays ~4–5% interest per year. Money is FDIC-insured up to $250k.

Risk: Effectively zero.

Good for: Your emergency fund and short-term goals (under 1–2 years).

Catch: Long-term it barely beats inflation. Don't put your "grow it" money here.

📈 Index Funds & ETFs

What: A single share that owns hundreds of companies (e.g. VOO, VTI = the S&P 500 / total US market).

Risk: Medium. Drops 20–40% in bad years, averages ~10%/yr historically.

Good for: Long-term wealth (5+ years). The boring choice that beats almost everything.

Catch: Boring. You won't get rich in a year.

🏢 Individual Stocks

What: Owning one company (Apple, Tesla, etc.).

Risk: High. A single bad earnings report and you're down 30%.

Good for: Learning, hobby money, or strong conviction picks (max ~10% of your portfolio).

Catch: Most beginners underperform a plain index fund. Don't bet rent money.

🪙 Crypto (BTC, ETH, etc.)

What: Decentralized digital assets. Bitcoin and Ethereum are the two most established.

Risk: Very high. 50%+ drops have happened multiple times. No FDIC. No earnings.

Good for: A small "speculative" slice (5–10% of portfolio max). Only money you can lose.

Catch: Avoid meme coins, "guaranteed" yield platforms, and influencer picks. Stick to BTC/ETH on a real exchange (Coinbase, Kraken). Never invest because it's "going to the moon".

🎓 Roth IRA

What: A retirement account you fund with after-tax money — withdrawals in retirement are 100% tax-free.

Risk: Depends on what you put in it (usually index funds).

Good for: Anyone with earned income. Starting at 19 instead of 29 can mean hundreds of thousands more at 65.

Catch: You need earned income (a job). 2026 limit: $7,000/yr. Money is locked until 59½ for max benefit.

💵 Bonds / Treasuries

What: You loan money to the government or a company; they pay you interest.

Risk: Low (especially US Treasuries).

Good for: Stability. Most students don't need many bonds yet — you have decades to ride out stock dips.

Catch: Returns are modest (~4–5%).

The 4 rules nobody told you

  1. Pay off high-interest debt first. Credit cards charge ~22%. No investment reliably beats that — paying off debt is a 22% return.
  2. Build a $500–$1,000 emergency fund in a HYSA before investing. Otherwise one car repair forces you to sell at a loss.
  3. Time in the market beats timing the market. Invest a fixed amount every month (dollar-cost averaging). Don't try to predict crashes.
  4. Fees and taxes matter. Use a free broker (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard). Avoid actively-managed funds with >0.5% expense ratios.

    Common student mistakes

    ❌ Chasing what's hot on TikTok

    By the time a stock or coin is trending, the easy money is gone. Influencers get paid to pump. Stick to your plan.

    ❌ Day trading with $200

    Even pros lose money trading. The math is brutal: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to break even. Just buy index funds.

    ❌ Putting all of it in crypto

    Crypto can 10x — and also -90%. A small slice is fine. All-in is gambling, not investing.

    ❌ Not investing because "I don't have enough"

    Fractional shares mean you can buy $5 of an ETF. Compound interest rewards starting early way more than starting big.

    ❌ Falling for "guaranteed returns"

    If a platform promises 15% guaranteed, it's a scam or about to collapse (see: Celsius, FTX, BlockFi). Real investing has no guarantees.

    ❌ Cashing out after a crash

    The biggest gains historically come right after the biggest drops. Selling low locks in losses. Keep buying through downturns.
